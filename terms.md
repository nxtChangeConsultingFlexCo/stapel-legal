## 2. What the app does

With Stapel you photograph receipts and invoices or hand them to the app as PDFs or images. The app
reads the receipt on your iPhone: issuer, date, total, tax rates and the individual line items. It
suggests a category, learns from your corrections, shows you month and year by category, issuer and
product, and tracks how prices of individual products develop. You can enter a warranty and a payment
deadline and be reminded of them. With Stapel Plus the app files the original as a searchable PDF into
a folder you choose in the Files app and exports your receipts as a CSV file.

Text recognition runs on your device. On iPhones with Apple Intelligence, Apple’s on-device language
model additionally helps. The app has no server and no account.

## 3. What the app does not promise

Recognition can make mistakes. An amount can be misread, a line item overlooked or a date mixed up.
The app therefore shows you whether the line items add up to the total, and you can change every
value. Check the values before you rely on them. We do not promise any particular recognition rate.

Stapel is an aid for your private overview. It is not bookkeeping, not an archive in the sense of tax
retention obligations and not tax or legal advice. Whether a PDF counts as proof towards authorities,
insurers or retailers depends on their rules. Whether and for how long a warranty or guarantee applies
follows from the purchase, not from the app.

Filing into your cloud folder depends on the respective service: if the folder is unreachable, the
provider’s app is not installed or the storage is full, the app cannot file and tells you so. If you connect OneDrive directly with your Microsoft account,
Microsoft is a separate provider with its own terms; whether OneDrive is available is up to Microsoft. iOS
shows reminders only if you have allowed notifications for Stapel.
